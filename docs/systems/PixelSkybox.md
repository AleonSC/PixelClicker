## PixelSkybox.cs

Sky styles (added by `PixelClicker.Awake`, implements `IPixelLookSource`): panoramas drawn in code (lat-long 2:1, same mapping as Unity's **Skybox/Panoramic** shader: u = 0.25 is +z) and set as `RenderSettings.skybox` (camera `clearFlags` -> Skybox; Classic restores the scene's skybox + camera background). Inspector list of `SkyStyle` (colours, sun elevation/azimuth/size, clouds, stars, width, pixelated, exposure, `rotateSpeed`, `flashesPerMinute`, seed; defaults by name): Classic, Clear Day, Sunset, Starry Night, Nebula, Aurora, Synthwave, Pixel Clouds, Storm (lightning). **Removed skies** (Synthwave, Alien World, Blocky Void, Black Hole, Spiral Galaxy, Ringed Planet: sky objects look wrong mirrored; `RemovedStyleNames` drops them from a saved scene list at Awake, their pattern code is dead). Panoramas and previews are cached per style (first pick of a big sky can hitch briefly). `panoramicShader` is filled in by `OnValidate` so builds keep the shader (else falls back to a solid camera colour + warning). `updateAmbientLight` (off) re-lights the scene from the sky. **Everything below the horizon is the sky mirrored (`Shade` flips `dir.y`), so every sky fills the whole view (the menu shows no floor).** **More skies** (`PixelSkyboxExtra.cs`, partial class, `ShadeExtra` / `ExtraStyles`, patterns appended to `SkyPattern`): weather (Rainy Day, Thunderstorm = Rain + flashes, Snowfall, Foggy Morning, Sandstorm, Rainbow Sky), space (Ringed Planet, Spiral Galaxy, Black Hole, Binary Suns) and cube world (Blocky Day / Night / Ember / Void: stepped gradients, square suns / moons, `BlockClouds`). **Watcher** (`PixelSkyboxEye.cs`, `SkyPattern.EyeVoid`, pitch black): in `LateUpdate` an eye (three code-textured quads far in front of the camera, so scene objects hide it) opens at a random spot in the upper sky, its slit pupil follows the cursor and it drifts a little towards it for `eyeWatchMin..Max` (6-14) s, blinks now and then, closes and stays away `eyeHiddenMin..Max` (10-28) s (`eyeSize` 0 = 0.16 of the screen height, `disableEye`; 0 = default). **More skies:** space variants without objects (6 `Nebula` colourings: Crimson / Emerald / Cobalt / Violet Haze / Golden Dust / Teal Void; `SkyPattern.Starfield` = Deep Space / Star Dust / Cold Stars, dense + coloured stars and a dusty band), Violet / Ice / Crimson Aurora, and **Underwater / Deep Ocean** (`SkyPattern.Underwater`, `UnderwaterColor`: caustic web overhead, swaying sun shafts, drifting specks, NOT mirrored; **animated**: `IsAnimated` styles are redrawn into their small (384 px) panorama about 12x a second from `Update` with `shadeTime` = `skyClock`, unscaled time, frozen in Time Stop). **Rain** (`PixelSkyboxRain.cs`, `UpdateRain`, `disableRain`, `rainPerSecond` 0 = 650 (x2 with lightning)): while a `Rain` pattern sky (Rainy Day, Thunderstorm) is on, a world-space stretched-billboard particle system falls through the scene, sized from the camera's distance to the cube. **The Watcher's eye** is fancier (`PixelSkyboxEye.cs`: heartbeat glow halo, bloodshot veins, fibrous iris with limbal ring / collarette / glints, a separate slit pupil that breathes and narrows when it stares at the cursor, lid line with tapering lashes). **Test Grid** (`SkyPattern.TestGrid`, `TestGridColor`): a NOT-mirrored black sky with a line every 15 degrees (thick every 45 azimuth / 30 elevation), the eight 45-degree azimuths in different colours (0 = +Z green, 90 = +X red), a white horizon, tinted below-horizon half, an orange disc (az 0 / el 30) and a white disc (az 90 / el 15) - for screenshots that diagnose orientation. **Sky icons** are their own little wide-angle pictures (`DrawPreview`: a 110-degree view towards the sun, tilted up, sampled through the same `Shade`; `PreviewRect` is the whole picture) instead of a crop of the panorama that missed most details. Picked in Settings (Display, "Sky" row) or the picker left of Play; saved in PlayerPrefs `PixelClicker.Setting.Skybox`.


**Shark (2026-10-16):** the Underwater / Deep Ocean skies draw a dark, hazy shark silhouette circling far below (`SharkShade`: directions below about 30 degrees down projected onto a flat sea-floor plane, a slow wobbly lap in about 26 s, swaying tail, fins) - only in the bottom of the sky, faded in from dir.y -0.5 to -0.72, never on the sides. It is part of the animated redraw (no extra objects).

**Glowing jellyfish (2026-10-16):** Deep Ocean only (by style name): a bioluminescent jellyfish (`JellyGlow`) drifts slowly round the view a little below the horizon; every 7 s it lights up - the bell's rim brightens, pulses of cyan / pink light run down its wavy tentacles - then fades back to a faint shape.
