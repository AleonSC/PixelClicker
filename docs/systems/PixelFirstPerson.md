# PixelFirstPerson

## PixelFirstPerson.cs

**First person mode** (added by `PixelClicker.Awake`; rebindable key `PixelAction.FirstPerson`, default F; `disableFirstPerson`): shrinks the player to `heightFraction` (0 = **1.5**, three times the old 0.5; walking and jumping scale with it via `SizeScale`) of an old pixel's edge and drops a `CharacterController` player on the floor in front of the cube (WASD / arrows, Shift run `runFactor`, Space jump `jumpHeight`, mouse look `lookSensitivity`, `walkSpeed` in old-pixel widths per second; unscaled time so it works during Time Stop; `PixelFirstPersonPush` **nudges** old pixels you run into: each contact only brings the pixel up towards `pushSpeed` (0 = 3 old-pixel widths per second, a fraction of the gap per contact), along your direction, no lift, spin damped, never a shove while you stand on it - nothing flies). **Carrying:** right-click a cube under the crosshair (`FindPickup`: nearest old pixel within `carryReach` (0 = 3.5 player heights) and a few degrees of the view) picks it up (`PickUp`: kinematic, colliders off, `OldPixelDespawn.Held`, gravity script off), up to `maxCarried` (0 = 3) stacked just in front of the chest (`PlaceCarried`, follows the view); right-click with nothing aimed at drops the top one (`DropTop`), left-click (not on the main cube) throws it (`ThrowTop`, `throwSpeed` 0 = 7 widths/s); leaving first person drops them (`DropAll`). `PixelFirstPerson.TryGetPlayerPosition` gives the player's spot (the floor edge fade centres on it). The normal camera stays where it is but its culling mask is set to 0 (so `PixelViewBounds`, fog, floor fitting and `Camera.main` users are unaffected) and a second camera (`CopyFrom` of it, near clip 6% of the player's height) at the eyes draws instead. **Left-click while the crosshair is on the cube, at any distance (`AimingAtCube`: angle to the cube vs its apparent radius + `aimAssistDegrees` 3, occlusion ignored), calls `PixelClicker.ManualClickFromAfar()`** (same rules as a mouse click: paused / intro lock / `minClickInterval`). `PixelFirstPerson.Active` makes `PixelClicker.ExternalClickBlock` read true (cube clicks, grabbing, device removal step aside), suppresses Time Slow's S key and the hose key, and a registered window (priority 120) makes Escape leave it; it can't start (or ends) during the title screen, pause, popups, visits, takeover minigames or while the hose is on. A crosshair (green when aiming at the cube) + a hint line are the HUD; cursor locked while playing and freed while paused. Windows are closed on entering (the cursor is locked, so UI can't be used inside).


**Update:** the F key is retired. First person is now the 'First person' tick box of the View window (`PixelViewWindow`, key V): `PixelFirstPerson.TryEnter()` / `Leave()`, Escape still leaves, and while the View window is open the mouse is free (the player doesn't look / move). First person is always the 3D view: `PixelOrthoView` forces perspective while it is active (`ForcePerspectiveNow` on entering).


## Build controls (attaching cubes) - replaces the old right-click carrying

**LEFT click** now grabs: aiming at a free old pixel (`FindPickup(false)`, within reach) picks it up, otherwise a left click aimed at the clicker still clicks it. You hold ONE cube straight ahead along the view (`PlaceCarried`). **Mouse wheel** moves it closer / farther (`holdDistance`, `wheelStep`), **holding the right button and moving the mouse** turns the cube instead of the view (`Look` -> `heldRotation`, `turnSensitivity`). When one of its faces is near another cube's face (`PixelAttachment.TrySnap`: within `attachAngle` of a grid-aligned orientation and `attachDistance` of a spot on the face, sliding along it in half-cube steps) a white pulsing ghost cube shows where it would stick. **Left click** then attaches it there (`AttachHeld`: a FixedJoint to the other cube, `PixelAttachment.Attach`, lifetime frozen so builds don't decay); with no ghost, left click sets the cube down. Attached cubes can't be picked up: **hold the right button on one** (`pryHoldSeconds`, a ring fills round the crosshair, `PixelHold`) to pry it loose (`PixelAttachment.Detach`: all its joints and the ones holding on to it). Throwing and the old stack of three are gone (`maxCarried` / `throwSpeed` are unused). Joints are not saved. Details: header of `PixelAttachment.cs`.

### Builds, foundations and prying (follow-up)
- Left click on a cube that is part of a build picks up the WHOLE build (PixelAttachment.Group; joints removed with TakeApart while carried, glued back on release). The wheel / right-hold turn work on the whole build; every carried cube has its own white ghost.
- A held cube or build can also snap flat onto the ground (PixelAttachment.TryGroundSnap, any of the 24 grid orientations relative to the world; the carried cube that must lift the most makes the contact). Clicking pins it with a world joint (AttachToWorld) so it stays as a foundation to build on.
- Prying (hold right on a cube of a build, ring on the crosshair via PixelHold's `at` argument) works again: FindPickup used to skip cubes whose lifetime is frozen, which every attached cube is.
