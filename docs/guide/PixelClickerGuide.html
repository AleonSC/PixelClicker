Pixel Clicker

Every click leaves something behind.

A 3D incremental clicker about a cube. Each click extracts a block's colour into your counter and drops its drained husk to the floor. Husks are material: you sort, store, carry and process them with machines and skills, until one clicking cube turns into a small busy world.

Cookie Clicker's numbers, RuneScape's skills, a dash of factory building, and a cube with a lot of personality.

Steam · ~$10 · one-time no story, no ads open-ended 100+ block types 8 skills 15+ minigames
01 · how a click works

The core loop

The cube shows one block at a time, picked at random (weighted) from the block types you have unlocked. Breaking it pays that block's own currency. The husk it drops is never currency by itself; it is the raw material the rest of the game runs on. Click a box to read what it does.

02 · block catalog

Every block on the cube

Each pixel type is its own currency and has a card in the catalog (tags, Pixel Guide text, husk behaviour). Filter by family, then pick a block to read its card.

03 · the first skill-tree choice

Block Hold and staking

Hold the right mouse button on the cube: the block floats out of the rotation while a meter drains (5 s; 10 s for hold blocks) and the cube rolls a new one so you keep clicking. Let go and it goes back next in line. Hold one of the six pure blocks for at least 0.75 s and you stake it: its value grows with time and with clicks during the hold, then a 60% roll decides whether it later pays the multiplier or nothing.

×1.00

Formula: 1 + 0.6 × seconds held + 0.1 per click during the hold. Recharge 8 s in the game; every 5 own clicks refill 1% of the meter.

Hold blocks

Some skill blocks are made to be held: the Whetstone turns your clicks into extra rapid clicks, Pesticide gives charges that clear Termite Hives. These are data rules in PixelBlockRules, so a new one is a single list entry.

04 · the heart of the game

Skills

Standard trains from every block. Gathering skills are chosen in the Skills window: the chosen pack replaces the basic rotation with its own blocks, and you train that skill. Each pack has a filler that shrinks as you level, a hazard, specials, perks and a tree. Skills are meant to feed each other.

06 · spending

Shop and economy

The shop opens on Cubie's second visit. Pure pixels (White, Gray, Black, Red, Green, Blue) are the shop currency. Tabs: Pixels, Upgrades (Features, Value, Operation), Consumables and Minigames. Numbers run with long suffixes up to about 1e306.

Value upgrade cost

Each pixel type has its own Value level, paid in that type: 25 × amount per click × 1.15level. A type's Value level can never pass the level of the skill that owns it (pure types are owned by Standard).

cost of the next level

Vendors

The Lumberjack and the Prospector buy skill blocks for pure pixels at a rarity rate (1 ÷ (spawn weight × amount per click ÷ clicks)) × 0.8 × luck, and sell devices and potions. Visitors offer their wares at 20% off.

Consumables

One potion per pixel type forces that type to spawn for a while. Crafting makes 2-type combo potions, 3-type ones (non-standalone types) and attuned devices (device + potion). Potions can be priced from their expected return.

07 · the husk economy

Devices and machines

Every device answers a pressure: despawning, floor space, hazards, the random rotation. Place them by right-clicking them in the Inventory. Factory machines (belts, kiln, splitter, elevator, robot) are tester features for now.

08 · interruptions worth having

Minigames

Bought as packs in the Minigames tab. Each one you switch on raises the global XP multiplier. Takeover minigames own the screen; PixelMinigameLimits caps how many run at once.

09 · everyone is a cube

Characters and pets

No story and no big reveal. The characters are ordinary people with small concerns, drawn in code as block-built cubes. When a visitor arrives the screen darkens and time stops until you accept or send them away.

10 · the long tail

Rare drops and collectibles

Rares sit at the end of the tier list and fill the Collection Log and a walkable gallery. The chart shows one chance in N per block (log scale); hover a bar for the expected time at 4 clicks a second.

11 · look, camera, interface

World and UI

World

A cube floating over a floor in a void. Floor styles, sky styles, day and night, screen filters, bar edge styles and a flat view. Per-type 3D looks (wood grain, ore cracks, glow, glass) live in PixelLooks, separate from each tier's UI colour.

Camera

A camera intro on start, then the View window (V): orbit, zoom, top-down, orthographic, free cam and first person, where you walk the floor, carry and stack husks and break them by hand. View bounds keep the camera in the world.

Interface

All UI is built in code with one shared font: HUD bars, Inventory, Log with goals, Toggles, Pocket with custom keybinds, tooltips, Pixel Guide, Game Database, notices and "Did you know" tips, a pause menu with save / load, and a title screen with music.

Sound

Code-made sounds and synths (PixelAudio), audio cues for minigames, skills, night, first person and the title screen, and the developer's own music tracks.

Saves

JSON in persistent data, per-save settings, autosave history with screenshots, and offline progress on load (today the auto clicker counts, plus Offline Progression XP).

Teaching

Cubie's visits, the shop and Toggles tutorials, the Standard level 2 popup that opens Skills, first-time tips, skill guides (Woodcutting first) and the Game Guide with Controls, How To Play and Database.

12 · game design document

Design direction

The world is ours: monotonous grinding for money, with a deliberately simulated feel. The pixels and numbers mean nothing in themselves; only the player gives them meaning. Cubie is just another guy.

Pillars

Every click leaves something behindValue and a physical husk on every click.
Each system answers a pressureDespawn, space, hazards, one skill at a time, the random rotation.
Skills feed each otherThe Whetstone from Mining for Woodcutting, Pesticide from Farming.
Surprise inside structureRandom when, designed what: you never know when a tree comes, but its shape is authored.
Played over daysShort, frequent sessions; the game keeps working while you are away.
Many ways to playManual, auto, machines, consumables, minigames. None is the only good one.
Charm everywhereThe vendors, the cats, the code-made sounds, the homages.
Decided
  • Audience: people who love numbers going up and collecting; RuneScape-type players first, idle fans too.
  • Prestige: paying Cubie back for his small gift and a second-hand Recycler, with interest he keeps inflating. A joke, not a theme.
  • Length: open-ended; pacing tuned per skill and goal.
  • 1.0: flesh out the systems, ship a handful of skills, add more as updates.
  • Art: smooth, clean menus with retro / 8-bit / cube visuals.
  • Early path: RGB is Cubie's gift at 100 Black; the Auto Clicker is the first buy; a skill is nudged as the first branch.
After 1.0, maybe
  • Gear: one slot ("you're a cube"), face sockets later; legendary gear from the super rares.
  • The alien vendor: a UFO at the floor's edge, reached in first person, selling relics.
  • Combat: enemies as tree-mode slot lists on the cube, weapons as gear.
  • First person as an unlock: the Cube Visor reveal. In 1.0 it stays simple.
